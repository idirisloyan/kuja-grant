The easiest end-to-end
grant system to comply with.

A donor defines a grant's criteria once. From there the system helps the NGO apply accurately, scores it fairly with AI and humans, and keeps everyone on top of every deliverable — offline, in five languages, on a cheap phone.

Finding (verified against the code): the Kuja tenant already implements most of what follows. The work ahead is consolidation, clarity, and polish — not net-new features. Each function below is tagged live partial gap.

01The core idea — one rubric, three consumers

Everything hinges on one fact: a grant's criteria are defined once, then power the whole lifecycle. That coherence is what makes our promise true rather than a slogan.

02Grant creation — two on-ramps, one wizard

Two ways in, converging on one editable wizard, then review-as-applicants-see-it, then publish.

ON-RAMP A · UPLOAD

Upload the call partial

Drop a PDF/DOC. AI extracts the skeleton and pre-fills the wizard; every item tagged ai_extracted / edited / manual and fully editable.

ON-RAMP B · GUIDED

Guided questions live

No document? The 6-step wizard asks plain questions — who can apply, what you'll fund, how you'll decide, what you need back and when — and builds the same grant.

CONVERGE

Review & publish live

See the grant as applicants will, confirm, publish. Fans out matches + alerts. Reusable criteria templates make repeat grants one click.

Real gap: today the upload pass extracts the reporting deliverables well; eligibility + weighted criteria + documents are filled by separate AI calls in the wizard. Improvement: one extraction that fills the whole skeleton — including deliverables with due dates — into the wizard for review.

04Scoring — AI and human, cleanly separated

The system already has three signals. The improvement is naming and clarity, so donors trust the result — not new capability.

Net: AI proposes and accelerates; humans decide; both are visible and comparable. The only real change is retiring the misleading ai_score field name and shaping the AI first-pass as a clean per-criterion object.

06Dashboards & UX — clean, uncluttered, action-oriented

One primary action per screen. Summary before detail. State shown in form — a chip, a due-soon stripe, an at-risk color — so what needs attention reads at a glance. The mockups above are the target visual direction.

On "the UI looks dated": the app is feature-complete but visually busy in places (tile sprawl, dense panels). The move is a design-system pass — the calm patterns shown here, redundant tiles retired, hierarchy and spacing modernized — not a rebuild. Prior work already started this (collapsed the NGO tile wall; "one obvious next action"; simplified the apply page).
